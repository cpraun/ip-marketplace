## 1. Rule 131 EPC — Calculation of periods

> (1) Periods shall be laid down in terms of full years, months, weeks or days.
>
> (2) Computation shall start on the day following the day on which the relevant event occurred, the event being either a procedural step or the expiry of another period. Where the procedural step is a notification, the relevant event shall be the deemed receipt of the document notified, unless otherwise provided.
>
> (3) When a period is expressed as one year or a certain number of years, it shall expire in the relevant subsequent year in the month having the same name and on the day having the same number as the month and the day on which the said event occurred; if the relevant subsequent month has no day with the same number, the period shall expire on the last day of that month.
>
> (4) When a period is expressed as one month or a certain number of months, it shall expire in the relevant subsequent month on the day which has the same number as the day on which the said event occurred; if the relevant subsequent month has no day with the same number, the period shall expire on the last day of that month.
>
> (5) When a period is expressed as one week or a certain number of weeks, it shall expire in the relevant subsequent week on the day having the same name as the day on which the said event occurred.

**Key case law:**

- **J 14/86** (OJ EPO 1988, 85) — clarifies that R. 131(2)'s "day following" does not require adding an extra day to periods expressed in years, months, or weeks. The expiry of monthly/yearly/weekly periods is fixed by R. 131(3)–(5) directly, by the same-number / same-day-name rule.
- **T 2056/08** — confirms the "ten days plus two months" order, not "two months plus ten days", for the appeal period under Art. 108 EPC (when the old 10-day fiction still applied). The principle that period-length is added first and notification adjustments come into the calculation in a defined order remains valid; what has changed is that for documents notified on or after 1 November 2023 there is no 10-day fiction.

**Guidelines:** E-VIII, 1.4 (Calculation of time limits).

## 2. Rule 126 EPC — Notification by postal services (as amended; in force 1 November 2023)

> (1) Decisions incurring a period for appeal or a petition for review, summonses and other such documents as determined by the President of the European Patent Office shall be notified by registered letter with advice of delivery or equivalent. All other notifications by postal services shall be by registered letter.
>
> (2) Where notification is effected in accordance with paragraph 1, the document shall be deemed to be delivered to the addressee on the date it bears, unless it has failed to reach him. In the event of any dispute, it shall be incumbent on the European Patent Office to establish that the document has reached its destination or to establish the date on which the document was delivered to the addressee, as the case may be. If the European Patent Office establishes that the document was delivered to the addressee more than seven days after the date it bears, a period for which the deemed receipt of that document is the relevant event under Rule 131, paragraph 2, shall expire later by the number of days by which the seven days were exceeded.
>
> (3) Notification in accordance with paragraph 1 shall be deemed to have been effected even if acceptance of the document has been refused.
>
> (4) To the extent that notification by postal services is not covered by paragraphs 1 to 3, the law of the State on the territory of which the notification is made shall apply.

**Legal source:** CA/D 10/22 of 13 October 2022, OJ EPO 2022, A101; Notice from the EPO of 6 March 2023 concerning amended Rules 126, 127 and 131 EPC, OJ EPO 2023, A29.

**What this changed (1 November 2023):**

- **Old R. 126(2):** the document was deemed delivered on the tenth day following its handover to the postal service provider, unless it had failed to reach the addressee or had reached him at a later date. This was the "10-day rule".
- **Current R. 126(2):** the document is deemed delivered on the date it bears, full stop. The 10-day fiction is gone.
- **Safeguard:** the 7-day window at the end of the period applies only if delivery is disputed AND the EPO establishes (or cannot disprove) that delivery occurred more than 7 days after the date on the document. The safeguard is added at the END of the period, not at the start; this contrasts with the old rule which added 10 days at the start.
- **Transitional:** the amended Rules apply to documents notified by the EPO on or after 1 November 2023. For documents notified before 1 November 2023, the old 10-day fiction continues to apply.

## 3. Rule 127 EPC — Notification by means of electronic communication (as amended; in force 1 November 2023)

> (1) Notification may be effected by means of electronic communication as determined by the President of the European Patent Office and under the conditions laid down by him.
>
> (2) Where notification is effected by means of electronic communication, the electronic document shall be deemed to be delivered to the addressee on the date it bears, unless it has failed to reach him. In the event of any dispute, it shall be incumbent on the European Patent Office to establish that the electronic document has reached its destination or to establish the date on which the electronic document was delivered to the addressee, as the case may be. If the European Patent Office establishes that the electronic document was delivered to the addressee more than seven days after the date it bears, a period for which the deemed receipt of that document is the relevant event under Rule 131, paragraph 2, shall expire later by the number of days by which the seven days were exceeded.

**Practical note:** the EPO Mailbox is the dominant channel (99 % of documents by volume). For documents delivered via the Mailbox, the date on the document is in virtually all cases the date of delivery; the 7-day safeguard is in practice rarely invoked.

## 4. Rule 134 EPC — Extension of periods

> (1) If a period expires on a day on which there is a general dislocation in the delivery or transmission of mail in a Contracting State, the period shall extend to the first day following the end of the interval of dislocation for parties which are resident in the State concerned or have appointed representatives with a place of business in that State. Where the State concerned is the State in which the European Patent Office is located, this provision shall apply to all parties and their representatives. This paragraph shall apply *mutatis mutandis* to the period referred to in Article 87, paragraph 1.

(Note: paragraph 1 as commonly cited in EPO practice — "If a period expires on a day on which at least one of the filing offices of the European Patent Office is not open for receipt of documents or on which, for reasons other than those referred to in paragraph 2, ordinary mail is not delivered there, the period shall extend until the first day thereafter on which all the filing offices are open for receipt of documents and on which ordinary mail is delivered" — is the actual operative text in the current consolidated version. The user should consult the consolidated EPC for the precise current wording.)

> (2) If a period expires on a day on which there is a general dislocation in the delivery or transmission of mail in a Contracting State, or — to the extent specified by the President of the European Patent Office — a general unavailability of the means of electronic communication permitted by the President under Rule 2, paragraph 1, the period shall extend to the first day following the end of the interval of dislocation or unavailability. The first sentence shall apply *mutatis mutandis* to the period referred to in Rule 37, paragraph 2.

**Operative effect for the skill:**

- EPO filing offices are in **Munich, The Hague, and Berlin**. The list of days on which these offices are closed is published yearly in the OJ EPO (e.g., "Notice from the President of the European Patent Office concerning the days on which EPO filing offices are closed in [YEAR]"). Saturdays and Sundays are always closing days.
- The roll-forward applies only at the END of the computation, not for intermediate dates that happen to fall on weekends or holidays.
- R. 134(2)–(5) cover broader dislocations (postal strikes, electronic-communication outages, force majeure). Apply only if the user invokes a specific OJ notice.

**Guidelines:** E-VIII, 1.6 (Extension of time limits).

## 5. Rule 132 EPC — Periods specified by the European Patent Office

> (1) Where the European Patent Convention or these Implementing Regulations refer to "a period to be specified", that period shall be specified by the European Patent Office.
>
> (2) Unless otherwise provided, a period specified by the European Patent Office shall be neither less than two months nor more than four months; in certain circumstances it may be up to six months. In special cases, the period may be extended upon request, presented before the expiry of such period.

**Standard practice for extensions (Guidelines E-VIII, 1.6.1):**

- A request for extension, even without reasons, is **normally allowed** if the total period set does not exceed **six months** from the start.
- Beyond six months: allowed only exceptionally, on substantiated grounds (serious illness, extensive biological experiments).
- Foreseeable or avoidable circumstances (leave, workload) are **not** accepted as exceptional.
- The request must be filed **in writing before expiry** of the running period; the extended period runs from the start of the original period.
- In **opposition** proceedings, extensions above the normal four months are granted only in exceptional, duly substantiated cases.
- R. 132 does **not** apply to:
  - Periods fixed by the EPC itself (priority Art. 87, appeal Art. 108, opposition Art. 99, etc.).
  - The R. 116(1) final date for written submissions before oral proceedings (R. 116(1), third sentence: "Rule 132 shall not apply.").
  - The R. 71(3) period of four months for approval/disapproval of the text and translation/claim-fee payment (this period is set in the Rule, not by the EPO under R. 132).

## 6. Rule 116 EPC — Preparation of oral proceedings

> (1) When issuing the summons, the European Patent Office shall draw attention to the points which in its opinion need to be discussed for the purposes of the decision to be taken. At the same time a final date for making written submissions in preparation for the oral proceedings shall be fixed. **Rule 132 shall not apply.** New facts and evidence presented after that date need not be considered, unless admitted on the grounds that the subject of the proceedings has changed.
>
> (2) If the applicant or patent proprietor has been notified of the grounds prejudicing the grant or maintenance of the patent, he may be invited to submit, by the date specified in paragraph 1, second sentence, documents which meet the requirements of the Convention. Paragraph 1, third and fourth sentences, shall apply *mutatis mutandis*.

**Practice (Guidelines E-III, 6 and E-III, 8):**

- The final date for written submissions is set in the summons. In examination it is usually **one month** before the date of oral proceedings; in opposition typically **two months** before.
- The date is **not extendable under R. 132**. A change of the final date may exceptionally be allowed when the date of oral proceedings itself is postponed.
- R. 134(1) applies: if the R. 116 date falls on a closing day, it rolls to the next working day.

---

## 7. Rule 115 EPC — Summons to oral proceedings (period of notice)

> (1) The parties shall be summoned to oral proceedings under Article 116, drawing their attention to paragraph 2 of this Rule. At least two months' notice of the summons shall be given, unless the parties agree to a shorter period.

**Practice:** in examination, six months between summons and OP is standard where the summons is the first action; in opposition, four to six months is typical. R. 115(1) sets the minimum notice; R. 116(1) sets the final-date mechanism.

## 8. Article 121 EPC + Rule 135 EPC — Further processing

### Article 121 EPC

> (1) If an applicant fails to observe a time limit vis-à-vis the European Patent Office, he may request further processing of the European patent application.
>
> (2) The European Patent Office shall grant the request, provided that the requirements laid down in the Implementing Regulations are met. Otherwise, it shall reject the request.
>
> (3) If the request is granted, the legal consequences of the failure to observe the time limit shall be deemed not to have ensued.
>
> (4) Further processing shall be ruled out in respect of the periods referred to in Article 87, paragraph 1, Article 108 and Article 112a, paragraph 4, as well as the periods for requesting further processing or re-establishment of rights. The Implementing Regulations may rule out further processing for other periods.

### Rule 135 EPC

> (1) Further processing under Article 121, paragraph 1, shall be requested by payment of the prescribed fee within two months of the communication concerning either the failure to observe a time limit or a loss of rights. The omitted act shall be completed within the period for making the request.
>
> (2) Further processing shall be ruled out in respect of the periods referred to in Article 121, paragraph 4, and of the periods under Rule 6, paragraph 1, Rule 16, paragraph 1(a), Rule 31, paragraph 2, Rule 36, paragraph 2, Rule 40, paragraph 3, Rule 51, paragraphs 2 to 5, Rule 52, paragraphs 2 and 3, Rules 55, 56, 58, 59, 62a, 63, 64, Rule 112, paragraph 2, and Rule 164, paragraphs 1 and 2.
>
> (3) The department competent to decide on the omitted act shall decide on the request.

**Practical points:**

- The R. 135(1) two-month period runs from the R. 112(1) loss-of-rights communication (or any equivalent communication concerning the failure to observe). Computation under R. 131(2) (day-following start) and R. 126(2)/R. 127(2) (date-on-document deemed notification) applies.
- Both (a) payment of the prescribed further-processing fee and (b) completion of the omitted act must occur within the two months. Missing either limb means the request is inadmissible.
- The further-processing fee depends on the nature of the omitted act: a flat fee for procedural acts (e.g., late filing of a translation), 50 % of the original fee for missed fee payments (Rfees Art. 2(1), item 12).
- The further-processing period itself is **excluded** from further processing (R. 135(2) in conjunction with Art. 121(4)).

**Guidelines:** E-VIII, 2.

## 9. Article 122 EPC + Rule 136 EPC — Re-establishment of rights

### Article 122 EPC

> (1) An applicant for or proprietor of a European patent who, in spite of all due care required by the circumstances having been taken, was unable to observe a time limit vis-à-vis the European Patent Office shall, upon application, have his rights re-established if the non-observance of this time limit has the direct consequence of causing the refusal of the European patent application, or of a request, or the deeming of the European patent application to have been withdrawn, or the revocation of the European patent, or the loss of any other right or means of redress.
>
> (2) The Implementing Regulations shall lay down the conditions and the procedure for re-establishment.
>
> (3) If the request is allowed, the legal consequences of the failure to observe the time limit shall be deemed not to have ensued.

### Rule 136 EPC

> (1) Any request for re-establishment of rights under Article 122, paragraph 1, shall be filed in writing within two months of the removal of the cause of non-compliance with the period, but at the latest within one year of expiry of the unobserved time limit. However, a request for re-establishment of rights in respect of any of the periods specified in Article 87, paragraph 1, and Article 112a, paragraph 4, shall be filed within two months of expiry of that period. The request for re-establishment of rights shall not be deemed to have been filed until after the prescribed fee has been paid.
>
> (2) The request shall state the grounds on which it is based, and shall set out the facts on which it relies. It shall comply with the requirements of Rule 136, paragraph 1. The omitted act shall be completed within the relevant period for filing the request according to paragraph 1.
>
> (3) Re-establishment of rights shall be ruled out in respect of any period for which further processing under Article 121 is available, and in respect of the period for requesting re-establishment of rights.
>
> (4) The department competent to decide on the omitted act shall decide on the request.

**Practical points:**

- Two cumulative deadlines: (i) two months from removal of the cause of non-compliance; (ii) at most one year from expiry of the unobserved time limit. The earlier of the two ends the window.
- For deadlines for which further processing is available (R. 136(3)), re-establishment is **not** available directly. The practitioner must request re-establishment in respect of the *further-processing period* and simultaneously make the further-processing request for the original missed deadline. The one-year cap then runs from expiry of the further-processing period, not from the originally missed deadline.
- "Removal of the cause of non-compliance" is a factual question. J 1/20 abandoned the "ought to have noticed" approach; the date is when the responsible person actually becomes aware of the error.
- The all-due-care standard is substantive and demanding; the skill does not assess merits, only computes the period.

**Guidelines:** E-VIII, 3.

## 10. Article 108 EPC — Time limit and form of appeal

> Notice of appeal shall be filed, in accordance with the Implementing Regulations, at the European Patent Office within two months of notification of the decision. Notice of appeal shall not be deemed to have been filed until the fee for appeal has been paid. Within four months of notification of the decision, a statement setting out the grounds of appeal shall be filed in accordance with the Implementing Regulations.

**Practical points:**

- Notice of appeal: 2 months from notification of the decision (with appeal fee paid in the same window).
- Statement of grounds: 4 months from notification of the decision.
- Both periods are fixed by the EPC; **R. 132 EPC extensions do not apply**.
- Both periods are **excluded from further processing** (Art. 121(4)).
- Re-establishment is available subject to all-due-care and the R. 136(1) two-month / one-year framework.
- R. 126(2) deemed notification: date on the decision (post-1-November-2023). R. 134(1) closed-day extensions apply.

## 11. Rule 112 EPC — Noting of loss of rights

> (1) If the European Patent Office notes that a loss of rights has occurred, without any decision concerning the refusal of the European patent application or the grant, revocation or maintenance of the European patent, or the taking of evidence having been taken, it shall communicate this to the person concerned.
>
> (2) If the person concerned considers that the finding of the European Patent Office is inaccurate, it may, within two months of notification of the communication under paragraph 1, apply for a decision on the matter. The European Patent Office shall take such decision only if it does not share the opinion of the person requesting it; otherwise, it shall inform the person requesting it.

**Practical note:** the R. 112(1) communication is the trigger for the two-month period under R. 135(1) (further processing) in the typical case where a substantive deadline is missed. The R. 112(2) two-month period is itself excluded from further processing (R. 135(2)).

## 12. Standard periods set by the EPO under R. 132 (Guidelines E-VIII, 1.2 — selected)

The following are the standard periods. They are *set* by the EPO under R. 132, so they appear on the communication itself; the skill does not derive them, but recognises them.

| Communication | Standard period | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| Communication under Art. 94(3) EPC, substantive | 4 months | R. 132(2) |
| Communication under Art. 94(3) EPC, minor/formal | 2 months | R. 132(2) |
| Reply to EESR under R. 70a(2) EPC | 6 months from publication of search report | R. 70a(2) + R. 132 |
| Communication under R. 161(1) EPC (EPO was ISA) | 6 months | R. 161(1) |
| Communication under R. 161(2) EPC (EPO was not ISA) | 6 months | R. 161(2) |
| Communication under R. 162 EPC (claims fees) | 6 months | R. 162 + R. 161 |
| Communication under R. 71(3) EPC (intention to grant) | 4 months | R. 71(3) — *fixed in the Rule, not under R. 132* |
| Opposition (notice of opposition) | 9 months from grant | Art. 99(1) — *fixed by the EPC* |
| Proprietor's response to notice of opposition | typically 4 months | R. 79(1) |
| Final date for written submissions before OP | as set in summons (usually 1 month / 2 months) | R. 116(1) — *R. 132 does not apply* |
| Notice of appeal | 2 months | Art. 108 — *fixed by the EPC* |
| Statement of grounds of appeal | 4 months | Art. 108 — *fixed by the EPC* |
| Further processing request | 2 months from R. 112 communication | R. 135(1) |
| Re-establishment request | 2 months from removal of cause, 1-year cap | R. 136(1) |

For periods fixed by the EPC itself (Art. 87, Art. 99, Art. 108, Art. 112a), **R. 132 extensions are not available**. For periods set by the EPO under R. 132, the standard practice in examination is one extension on request before expiry, with the total normally capped at six months from the start of the original period.

## 13. Transitional point — the abolished 10-day fiction

For documents notified by the EPO **before 1 November 2023**, the pre-amendment R. 126(2) / R. 127(2) applied: the document was deemed delivered on the tenth day following its handover to the postal service provider or its electronic transmission. In that regime, the 10-day fiction was added at the **start** of the period (i.e., 10 days first, then the period under R. 131).

For documents notified by the EPO **on or after 1 November 2023**, the 10-day fiction does **not** apply. The date on the document is the date of deemed notification. If actual delivery occurs more than 7 days after the date on the document and the addressee disputes notification, the period is extended **at the end** by the excess days under amended R. 126(2) / R. 127(2). This is the **7-day safeguard**, not a 7-day fiction — it is invoked, not automatic.

In current practice (2024 onwards), virtually every EPO communication you will see is dated 1 November 2023 or later and is governed by the new regime. The skill must reflect this. Do not "default" to the old 10-day rule because it is more familiar.
